# Art direction reference

## Source analysis

For each shortlisted image, identify:

- semantic nucleus;
- dominant gesture or path;
- figure-ground structure;
- native palette and meaningful minor color;
- material behavior such as fog, water, stone, foliage, fabric, or light;
- useful negative space;
- distracting reality to remove;
- emotional residue after factual description is removed.

## Pre-edit decisions

Crop around the dominant gesture rather than a standard rule of thirds. A small rotation is justified when it strengthens an existing slope, current, road, or horizon. Preserve atmospheric softness; do not make fog, rain, or distance unnaturally crisp.

Separate factual correction from artistic transformation. Save a corrected master before generation.

## Composition families

- **Photographic window:** one intact image region surrounded by authored material.
- **Diagonal incision:** a road, river, ridge, or tear carries the eye across the frame.
- **Stratified field:** water, shore, forest, or sky becomes horizontal material bands.
- **Irregular fragments:** two to four source crops establish rhythm without dense scrapbooking.
- **Large quiet plane:** one small anchor balances substantial paper or atmospheric space.
- **Material crossing:** a thread, current, contour, or cloud passes between photographic and fabricated regions.
- **Type as structure:** one short word becomes a block, seam, counterweight, or submerged layer.

## Material logic

Select a process because it resembles or contradicts the source:

- wax for fog, memory, concealment, and layered weather;
- resin or glass for water, stones, preservation, and suspended depth;
- fiber or stitching for paths, flags, continuity, and tension;
- cyanotype for sky, exposure, botany, and weather records;
- radiograph for veins, stems, hidden structure, and fragile growth;
- ceramic relief for clouds, mountains, weight, and permanence;
- graphite, embossing, or foil for geology, roads, towers, and technical scale;
- mineral pigment for sediment, sunset, atmospheric residue, and abstraction;
- torn paper for rupture, passage, missing information, and spatial transition.

Use one primary material and at most two supporting materials. Vary scale, edge, depth, and placement across the series.

## Color

Start from the source palette. Use one strong color event when the photograph contains a meaningful flag, flower, path, sky, sign, or mineral hue. Preserve high chroma only where it controls the eye path.

Across nine images, alternate warm, cool, neutral, and high-chroma moments. Avoid nine beige-paper works or nine cobalt works unless a monochrome series was requested.

## Typography

Text is optional. Decide whether it acts as title, counterweight, seam, interruption, or surface texture.

- Prefer zero to four words.
- Use exact supplied wording when available.
- Use generic but truthful language when the location is uncertain.
- Never invent a place name, date, altitude, or historical claim.
- Mix type scale and placement across the series; do not put every caption in the same corner.
- If the model corrupts essential text, regenerate or remove it.

## Prompt architecture

Write prompts in this order:

1. use case and output role;
2. reference-image roles;
3. preserved source anchors;
4. composition and photographic share;
5. primary and supporting materials;
6. color event and text, if any;
7. exclusions and aspect ratio.

State visible consequences rather than style adjectives. Replace “artistic” with exact instructions such as “retain one crisp photographic river strip, tear it open at the center, and continue the water inside a clear resin channel.”
